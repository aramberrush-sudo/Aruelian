Pre-Listing Repairs in Henderson: A Winter Prep Guide

Published November 24, 2026 · Aruelian Home Services

If you’re thinking about selling in the spring, the weeks between Thanksgiving and the new year are a quiet, comfortable window to get the house ready. The weather is mild, you aren’t racing a listing date yet, and the small repairs that make a home show well can be handled on your schedule instead of in a rush.

Most pre-listing work isn’t a remodel. It’s a punch list: the dings, drips, sticky doors and tired caulk that you’ve learned to step around but a buyer and their inspector will notice right away. Here’s how to approach it, and what usually belongs on the list for a Henderson or Las Vegas home.

Walk the house like a buyer would

Buyers open everything. Before you make a list, do the same: open every door, cabinet and drawer, flip every switch, run every faucet and flush every toilet. Look up at ceilings and down at baseboards. Stand in each doorway and notice what catches your eye first.

Write each item down with its room and a photo. If your agent has already walked the house, add their notes to the same list so everything lives in one place. One complete list is what makes the rest of this go quickly.

Fix what an inspector is likely to note

Every home inspection is different, but some small items come up again and again in valley homes. Handling them before you list can mean fewer surprises in the repair-request stage. Common examples:

  • Running toilets and slow drips. A worn flapper, a dripping faucet handle or a damp spot under a sink cabinet.
  • Missing or cracked caulk around tubs, showers, sinks and backsplashes. Our dry summers shrink caulk and pull it away from surfaces.
  • Doors that don’t latch or rub the frame, and missing door stops that have let knobs punch into drywall.
  • Loose fixtures and hardware. Wobbly towel bars, loose toilets, cabinet doors that sag, a ceiling fan that shakes on high.
  • Worn weatherstripping and door sweeps on exterior doors and the garage entry door. Light or dust coming under a door is an easy tell.
  • Burned-out bulbs and dead smoke detector batteries. Small, but they make a house look neglected in photos and at showings.

Some items are bigger than they look, like electrical changes, moving plumbing, roof leaks or HVAC problems. Walls and paint: the biggest visual return

Listing photos are unforgiving about walls. Scuffs, nail holes from family photos, patched spots that never got painted and cracked corner bead all show up. Two practical points for Southern Nevada homes:

  • Match the texture. Many homes here have knockdown or orange-peel texture. A smooth patch on a textured wall stands out even after paint, so texture matching matters as much as the patch itself.
  • Touch-up versus full repaint. If you have the original paint and it hasn’t faded, touch-ups can work. Often a room looks better with one fresh coat wall to wall, especially in high-traffic halls and kids’ rooms. Neutral colors photograph well and let buyers picture their own things.

Small updates that help a house show

You don’t need to remodel the kitchen to sell. A few like-for-like swaps can make dated spaces feel cared for:

  • Replacing a dated light fixture or builder-grade vanity light.
  • New cabinet pulls and hinges where the old ones are worn or mismatched.
  • A new faucet or showerhead where hard-water scale has left finishes spotty.
  • Fresh grout cleaning or sealing and new caulk in the bathrooms.
  • Outlet and switch cover plates that match throughout.

Talk with your agent before spending on anything bigger. They know what buyers in your neighborhood expect and which projects are worth it for your price range.

Don’t skip the outside and the garage

First impressions start at the curb. Check the front door (paint, hardware, the threshold and the sweep), house numbers, exterior light fixtures and the gate. Look at the garage too: a stuck side door, a loose garage-entry door handle or damaged drywall in the garage is easy to fix and easy for a buyer to notice.

Bundle it into one handyman visit

The most efficient way through a pre-listing punch list is to hand the whole thing over at once. Group items by room, mark the must-dos, note which fixtures or paint you already have, and share any HOA work-hour rules or gate codes. A handyman in Henderson or Las Vegas can then plan materials and get through the list in as few trips as possible, which matters when you’re also decluttering, staging and scheduling photos.
