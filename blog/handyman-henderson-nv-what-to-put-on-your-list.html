Handyman in Henderson NV: What to Put on Your List

Published November 17, 2026 · Aruelian Home Services

Most homes don’t have one big problem. They have fifteen small ones: a door that won’t latch, a nail hole from last year’s art, a toilet that runs at night, a light fixture you bought months ago and never hung. The fastest way to get them handled is to write them all down and take care of them in one visit instead of five.

Mid-November is a good time to do it. The heat is gone, it’s comfortable to work with doors and windows open, and the holidays put guests in your guest bath and on your couch. Here’s how to build a list a handyman in Henderson or Las Vegas can actually work from, and what tends to belong on it.

Start with a room-by-room walk

Grab your phone and walk the house in order: entry, living areas, kitchen, each bathroom, bedrooms, laundry, garage, then the exterior. In each room, open and close every door and drawer, flip every switch, and run every faucet. Write down anything that sticks, wobbles, drips, flickers or looks rough.

For each item, take a quick photo and add a note: where it is, what’s wrong, and anything you already have on hand (the replacement faucet in the garage, the leftover paint can, the TV mount still in its box). Measurements help too, like the width of the wall where shelves should go. A list with photos lets someone plan materials and time before they show up.

Walls, doors and hardware

  • Drywall dings and holes. Nail holes, doorknob dents, anchor holes from old shelves and corner bead that’s cracked. Note the wall texture, since many valley homes have knockdown or orange-peel texture that needs to be matched before paint. If you have the original paint, say so.
  • Sticking or drifting doors. Doors that rub, won’t latch or swing open on their own often need hinge screws tightened, a strike plate adjusted or a hinge shimmed.
  • Loose hardware. Wobbly cabinet pulls, sagging cabinet doors, towel bars pulling out of the wall, missing door stops.
  • Mounting and hanging. TVs, floating shelves, mirrors, curtain rods and art. Note what’s going up and roughly how heavy it is so the right anchors or stud locations can be planned.

Desert-specific items people forget

Southern Nevada is hard on a house in its own way. Summer heat dries out and shrinks caulk and weatherstripping, wind pushes fine dust through every gap, and hard water leaves mineral buildup on anything wet. Add these to the list:

  • Weatherstripping and door sweeps on exterior doors, including the door from the garage. If you can see daylight around the door or find a line of dust on the floor inside it, it’s worn.
  • Caulking around tubs, showers, sinks, backsplashes and window trim. Cracked or pulled-away caulk lets water and dust in.
  • Faucet aerators and showerheads with weak or uneven flow from scale.
  • Running toilets, which are usually a worn flapper or fill valve.
  • Slow drips under sinks and at faucet handles, plus a garbage disposal that hums but won’t turn.

Fixtures and small swaps

Like-for-like swaps are some of the most satisfying items to cross off: a dated light fixture, a ceiling fan that wobbles, a worn-out faucet, cabinet hinges, a new toilet seat, smoke detector batteries. Note whether you already bought the replacement and whether the old one works at all.

Some items look small but aren’t handyman work, such as adding a new circuit or outlet where there wasn’t one, moving plumbing lines, structural changes, HVAC and roofing. Bundle it into one visit

Ten small jobs in one trip usually go much more smoothly than ten separate calls. To make that visit efficient:

  • Rank the list. Mark the must-dos (anything leaking, anything a guest will touch) so they get done first.
  • Group by room. It cuts down on setup and cleanup time.
  • Decide on materials. Note which fixtures and finishes you’re supplying and which you want picked up.
  • Share access details. Gate codes, HOA rules for work hours, parking and pets.

The same approach works for a pre-listing punch list, a rental turnover or a move-in list. It’s the same walk-through with a different deadline.
